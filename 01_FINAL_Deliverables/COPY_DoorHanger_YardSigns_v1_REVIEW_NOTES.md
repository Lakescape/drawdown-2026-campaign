## LAYER 5 — DEVIL'S ADVOCATE RED TEAM

### Five specific weaknesses (found and addressed)

- **W1 — Inherited unhedged assertion.** The source draft's hanger headline ("The lake is dropping. Is your shoreline ready?") asserts the drawdown as fact — the exact banned construction. On print, an unhedged claim that the City then contradicts is worse than a bad call script: it's physical, photographable, and forwardable. **Fix:** all headlines rebuilt on approved hedges ("being lined up," "projected," "pointing to").
- **W2 — Live scarcity on a static medium.** The source draft's "Only [X] assessment slots remaining" is unfillable on print — the count moves twice a week; the paper never does. First draft inherited it; Sales Engineer voted WEAK on exactly this. **Fix:** scarcity reduced to the approved structural set only (2 machines, ~25 working days, committed in July).
- **W3 — Competitor-incapacity tagline.** "We work the lake bed. Others work from the shore." is a competitor claim wearing a brand line's clothes — banned by the same hard rule the Call Script enforces. **Fix:** cut to "We work the lake bed." (Yard Sign Option 6).
- **W4 — Testimonial placeholder could ship as a fake.** The source draft's testimonial is a bracketed invention. On a 500-unit run into $2M+ streets, a fabricated or anonymous quote is a reputational worst case. **Fix:** Global Rule 6 + no-testimonial variant layout (hanger spec 7). No approved real quote by print day → the variant prints.
- **W5 — "Your neighbors booked" is a verifiable factual claim.** Lake Austin coves are small social graphs; if the sign is placed where bookings don't exist, the first dog-walker who asks a neighbor detonates the campaign's credibility in that cove. **Fix:** Option 5 gated on ≥3 confirmed bookings in that cove at the Mon/Thu standup, with a pull rule when the claim goes stale.
- **W6 (additional) — QR-to-homepage attribution leak.** First draft pointed the QR at the homepage: no attribution, no campaign continuity, and a $5M homeowner landing on a generic page. **Fix:** dedicated booking URL + UTM, scan-tested proof; ownership gap flagged.

### Three concrete improvements (applied)

1. **Dates quarantined to the reprintable piece.** Yard signs carry no dates at all (season-scale "this fall" maximum, Option 2); only the door hanger names October–November, and the run is split 250/250 so a window shift costs half a print run, not a full one.
2. **Deployment gating as copy, not commentary.** Option 5's condition is written into the deliverable as an operational rule tied to the standup rhythm — the same Mon/Thu cadence that governs live counts everywhere else in the campaign.
3. **Kill-switch written for print specifically.** Other deliverables' kill-switches assume digital recall. Print can't be recalled — so the kill-switch here is: stop drops, let hedged field pieces ride, reprint only run two. Every line was re-checked against that scenario.

### Worst-case scenario

The City formally shifts the window to spring in mid-September — after 500 hangers are on doors and 30 signs are in yards, and two weeks before the referral program was set to launch. **Survival check:** every piece in the field still reads true ("being lined up," "exploring," "pointing to," 12-month credit, make-it-right promise). No dates on signs to contradict. The hanger's October–November pointer is stale but framed as projection; the second 250-run reprints with the new pointer. The sales floor pivots to the Email Suite's kill-switch language, and the print in the field becomes supporting evidence of the carry-over message instead of a liability. **Verdict: survivable by design — this is why the hedging rules exist.**
