## GLOBAL RULES (READ BEFORE THESE GO LIVE)

1. **A human answers, not a bot.** These responses are typed (or pasted and personalized) by a trained rep inside the chat SLA. If the chat tool auto-serves any of them verbatim, strip the brackets first — a visible placeholder in chat is worse than a slow reply.
2. **Two sentences, then a booking question.** Every answer below is built that way on purpose. Do not stack a second answer onto one reply — if they ask two things, answer one and ask which to take first.
3. **Hedged drawdown language only.** The City is "exploring" the drawdown; work is "lined up" / "projected" / "pointing to October–November." Never write that the drawdown "is happening," and never state unhedged dates. We sell preparation, not prophecy.
4. **Real scarcity only.** Approved structural numbers: **2 amphibious machines locked, ~25 working days in-window.** If a chatter pushes for a live slot count, quote it only as "as of [Monday/Thursday]'s count" from the published number — anything unknown is an [X] placeholder, never improvised.
5. **We state what we locked, never what competitors can't get.** "We committed in July" — yes. "No one else can get these machines" — no.
6. **Pricing is ranges, hedged as ranges.** $15–25K / $35–55K / $65–95K only, always with "depends on what we find" attached. Never quote a package price as firm in chat, and never quote a group discount percentage — group terms come from Nathan, in writing.
7. **Consistency with the phone.** These answers say the same things the call script says, in fewer words. If a chat gets past two exchanges, the rep's job is to move it to a call or a booking — chat is a front porch, not the house.

## THE 7 FAQ RESPONSES

### 1. "What is the Priority Assessment?"

> It's an on-site evaluation of your shoreline — bulkhead, sediment depth, dock structure, and drainage — with photo documentation, a written prioritized scope, and rough pricing. It's $695, and every dollar of it credits toward any work you book. Want me to get you on the Assessment calendar?

### 2. "How much does the work cost?"

> Honest answer: it depends on what we find — most smaller jobs run $15–25K, typical bulkhead-and-sediment packages usually land in the $35–55K range, and full cove work can reach $65–95K. The Assessment gives you your real number instead of a guess, and the $695 credits toward it. Want me to set one up so you know what you're actually dealing with?

### 3. "When is the drawdown happening?"

> LCRA announced it — the drawdown is happening, and the work being lined up points to October–November, the first meaningful one in nearly a decade. The exact window is not fixed yet, and we prepare ahead of it because equipment and calendar have to be locked months out, not the week the water drops. Want me to hold an Assessment slot so you're ready either way?

### 4. "Can I get a quote without the Assessment?"

> I can give you ranges, but without seeing your sediment depth and structure any number I throw out would be a guess — and a wrong number helps neither of us. The $695 is fully credited, so if you move forward, the Assessment costs you nothing. Want to start there?

### 5. "What if the drawdown is delayed?"

> Nothing is lost — your $695 credit stays valid for 12 months, and your place in the calendar carries over if the window shifts. Moving early doesn't bet on the exact dates; it just keeps you at the front of the line whatever the dates turn out to be. Want me to hold a slot while they're still open?

### 6. "Do you work with HOAs?"

> All the time — we work with HOAs all over Lake Austin, and I can put together a board packet or present at a meeting myself. Grouped properties share equipment mobilization, which brings the per-property cost down, and the $695-per-property Assessment is still fully credited. Want me to set up a call with you and your board contact?

### 7. "How do I book?"

> Right here works — tell me a day that suits you and I'll put you on the calendar, or call or text us directly at [rep chat-escalation number]. If you'd rather ease into it, send a few photos of your shoreline and I'll tell you honestly whether a full Assessment is worth your $695. What day works for you?

## HOW TO RUN LIVE CHAT (USAGE BLOCK)

1. **Response time is the product.** Chat inquiries route to a rep inside the 5-minute SLA, same as SMS replies. A $5M homeowner who gets an instant, human, two-sentence answer has already been sold on how we operate.
2. **Move up the ladder fast.** One or two exchanges in chat, then the rep offers the booking, a call, or the photo-assessment path (FAQ 7's second sentence). Chat threads longer than five messages without a next step are a leak — the rep closes for the calendar or the call.
3. **Never let chat outrun standup truth.** If equipment status moves to the floor case (1 machine, 20 days), any scarcity language in chat matches it. Live slot counts come only from the Mon/Thu published number, quoted as "as of [day]'s count."
4. **Escalate to Nathan when:** a high-ticket prospect (full cove potential) wants pricing negotiation, an HOA wants group terms in writing, or anyone mentions a structural emergency — active collapse or safety hazard gets flagged same-day regardless of window. (Same triggers as the call script, Hard Rule 7.)
5. **Kill-switch (drawdown delay/cancellation).** If the City/LCRA shifts the window, FAQ 3 and FAQ 5's framing is already built for it — "exploring" and "carries over" stay true. Reps lead with: nothing is lost, credits and calendar places carry over, and the chatter keeps their place in line.
6. **Log everything to the CRM.** Every chat ends in a booked Assessment, a scheduled next touch with a date, or a clean exit — the same three outcomes as a call. Chats with contact info go into the same nurture tracks; suppression requests are honored everywhere, permanently.
