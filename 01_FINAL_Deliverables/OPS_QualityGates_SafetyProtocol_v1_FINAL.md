## B.3 — Emergency Procedures (6 scenarios)

**Universal first moves for ALL scenarios:** Stop work → account for every person → call 911 if life/safety is at stake → notify Operations Lead and Nathan → secure the scene → document (photos + written log) before leaving. Emergencies are announced in #drawdown-ops with job ID.

### 1. Man Overboard
1. **Throw, don't go:** throw the staged ring/pole — do not enter the water unless trained and tethered. A second person in the water doubles the emergency.
2. Call 911 immediately if the person is not recovered within seconds, is injured, or is unresponsive.
3. Keep eyes on the person; assign one spotter who never looks away.
4. If recovered: first aid, treat as medical emergency, monitor for delayed drowning symptoms; medical evaluation mandatory even if apparently fine [X — per Safety Officer protocol].
5. Notify Operations Lead + Nathan. Incident report within 24 hours. Work does not resume at that site same day.

### 2. Equipment Hydraulic Failure on Water (amphibious unit / barge)
1. Operator shuts down and stays with the machine if it is stable; evacuates to PFD + barge/shore if listing or taking on water.
2. All personnel move clear of pinch/crush zones and hydraulic lines under pressure — **never** touch a suspected burst line.
3. Call 911 if anyone is injured or the machine endangers people/property; then Operations Lead + Nathan.
4. Do not attempt field repair of pressurized hydraulics. Unit is towed/recovered by qualified recovery service [X — recovery vendor on retainer, contact in emergency contacts template].
5. Scene photographed before recovery. Incident report within 24 hours. Unit tagged out until inspected by qualified mechanic.

### 3. Heat Illness
1. Recognize: cramps → exhaustion (heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness) → stroke (hot dry skin, confusion, collapse = **call 911 immediately**).
2. Move person to shade/cooling, loosen clothing, cool with water/ice packs to neck/armpits/groin, give water only if fully alert.
3. Heat stroke is a 911 call every time — do not "wait and see."
4. Prevention rule in effect Oct–Nov [X — confirm seasonal applicability with Safety Officer]: scheduled shade/water breaks, buddy check every hour, work/rest cycle per general OSHA heat guidance [X — specific thresholds set by Safety Officer].
5. Any heat illness event = incident report + remainder-of-day removal for the affected person.

### 4. Severe Weather / Lightning
1. Thunder heard or lightning seen = **stop work immediately**, lower booms/attachments, exit the water and amphibious units, take shelter in vehicles or a substantial building.
2. Work resumes no sooner than 30 minutes after the last thunder/lightning (general NOAA lightning-safety guidance).
3. High wind, flash-flood advisory, or rapidly rising water = same stop-work rule; secure equipment above the waterline before sheltering.
4. Operations Lead owns the resume call; buffer-day protocol applies (Playbook §4, §12).
5. Any lightning strike or near-miss = 911 as needed + incident report.

### 5. Underwater Hazard Strike (piling, rebar, utility, debris)
1. Stop the machine immediately — no second strike. Hold position; do not withdraw tooling if doing so could worsen damage (e.g., snagged utility).
2. If a struck utility is suspected (gas smell, sparking, water main): evacuate upslope, call 911 and the utility, keep the public clear [X — utility emergency numbers in contacts template].
3. If hull/pontoon damage is suspected, execute Scenario 2 (stability check, PFDs, evacuate if listing).
4. Mark and photograph the hazard location before any further work; update the job hazard map.
5. Notify Operations Lead + Nathan. Struck-utility events also notify the locate service (811 ticket reference from Gate 1.8). Incident report within 24 hours.

### 6. Injury / Medical Evacuation (Medevac)
1. Stop work; one person leads first aid, one person calls 911, one person guides EMS to the site (water access points are confusing — send a guide to the road).
2. Do not move a person with suspected head/neck/spinal injury unless the scene is unsafe.
3. Nearest emergency room and water-access rendezvous points per job packet [X — pre-mapped per cove by Safety Officer before mobilization].
4. Notify Operations Lead + Nathan immediately after 911.
5. Scene preserved for documentation; incident report within 24 hours; OSHA recordability review by Safety Officer [X — qualified safety officer confirms reporting obligations].
