## 5. LAYER 5 — DEVIL'S ADVOCATE RED TEAM

**Weaknesses found (6):**

1. **Lingering print risk.** If the drawdown is cancelled after these are printed, the document keeps circulating. *Mitigation:* every date claim is hedged ("exploring," "projected," "lined up"), and the footer explicitly addresses a window shift — the document is written to remain true in the kill-switch scenario. Residual risk accepted; it is inherent to any printed asset.
2. **Placeholder printing.** A rep could print the file with the QR box and phone line unfilled. *Mitigation:* both placeholders are self-describing and visually unfinished (dashed border, bracketed caps); the production gate is stated in the HTML header comment and this file's status line. *Recommendation:* Operations fills them once and distributes only the filled file.
3. **Price anchoring cuts both ways.** A small-job owner may read $15K as the floor and self-select out; a full-cove owner may anchor at $95K. *Mitigation:* "depending on scope" + "rough order-of-magnitude pricing" in the report. Accepted — the alternative (no numbers) reads as evasive to a $5M homeowner.
4. **"30–40% less expensive" is the boldest number on the page.** *Mitigation:* it is verbatim-approved in Call Script Rebuttal #1 and carries "typically." If ever challenged, the defense is the full-water method (barges, blind work) described in the same paragraph.
5. **"Eight to ten years" is unfalsifiable-ish.** A skeptic could call it scare arithmetic. *Mitigation:* "may be," sourced to lake history, and consistent across every approved asset — consistency is what survives a screenshot.
6. **One page is tight.** Adding anything (testimonials, photos, group terms) breaks the one-page discipline. *Mitigation:* nothing added; the short Assessment video (Deliverable 2.2) remains the companion asset per Rebuttal 10.

**Concrete improvements applied (3):**
1. QR placeholder made instructional and visually unfinished (dashed border, "[QR CODE] — insert before print").
2. Window-shift credit language duplicated in the footer so it survives even if a reader only scans the page top-to-bottom once.
3. Grayscale-safe hierarchy verified — no information is carried by color alone.

**Worst-case scenario (1):** The City cancels the drawdown in September; a homeowner forwards the one-pager to their HOA list with "this company is fear-mongering." *Defense:* the document never says the drawdown is happening — it says the City is exploring it, the work is being lined up, and we are preparing as if it's coming; it says the credit holds 12 months if the window shifts; and it offers certainty ("if everything's sound, we'll tell you to your face") rather than catastrophe. In the worst case the document reads as prudent preparation, not prophecy. That is the standard it was written to.
