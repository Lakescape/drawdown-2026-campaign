## LAYER 5 — DEVIL'S ADVOCATE RED TEAM

### Five specific weaknesses (found and addressed)

- **W1 — "First in line" was an unenforceable promise.** The pre-launch pitch promises priority when the program activates, but the draft had no mechanism making that true. A rep who logs the referral "when they get a minute" has broken a promise to a client who did us a favor. **Fix:** Usage rule 3 (same-day Linear logging with named fields) and Usage rule 4 (Oct 15 launch-day sweep — every pre-launch referral gets a personal message that the program is live and their name carried over).
- **W2 — Version gate ambiguity at the Oct 15 boundary.** Gating on job-completion date strands jobs completed Oct 10–14: their emails send after the program is live, carrying pre-launch language that contradicts reality. **Fix:** Global Rule 1 keys the gate to the send/delivery date and resolves the gray zone explicitly.
- **W3 — The in-person ask could land as payment leverage.** A referral request delivered while the client is signing off on final payment reads as "the invoice comes with strings." On Lake Austin's small social graph, one such story costs more than any referral earns. **Fix:** the beat is structurally separated from the transaction — banned during the invoice conversation, banned with any open punch-list item, gated on the client volunteering satisfaction.
- **W4 — "Launches in October" is a date claim we control.** Unlike the drawdown, the program launch is our own commitment — so hedging rules don't protect it; only keeping it does. **Fix:** Usage rule 10 kill-switch: if the date moves, revert to pre-launch wording with "launches later this fall" and Nathan re-confirms before anyone quotes a month again.
- **W5 — The post-launch text risked a transactional, bounty-hunter tone.** "$500 for a name" compressed into 160 characters can strip the warmth that makes the ask acceptable. **Fix:** beat order preserved even at character cost (gratitude → ask → credit → exit), credit framed as "our way of saying thanks" in the long-form versions, and the compression trade documented in the deliverable itself.
- **W6 (additional) — GSM-7 drift.** The first draft's texts counted over 160 and carried smart-quote risk from word-processor composition. **Fix:** both texts machine-verified (T-A 148 raw / 154 worst-case; T-B 153 raw / 159 worst-case), and Global Rule 7 mirrors the SMS suite's type-directly-into-the-platform rule.

### Three concrete improvements (applied)

1. **Launch-day sweep written as a scheduled operation** (Usage rule 4), not an aspiration — the pre-launch promise now has a dated keeper.
2. **Permission-to-mention added to the in-person script and the Linear field set** ("Want me to say you sent me, or keep your name out of it?") — protects quiet referrers and the referred prospect's Scenario 3 handling.
3. **Neighbor-logic rationale added to speaker notes** ("grouped properties on the same cove share equipment time — saves everyone money"), giving referrers a non-monetary reason that passes the BBQ test and matches Call Script Scenario 1 Branch 1E exactly.

### Worst-case scenario

A $5M client refers their closest neighbor in late September (pre-launch). The rep logs it a week late; the program launches Oct 15; the referred neighbor gets a Scenario 3 call, but the referrer never hears about the credit — then receives someone else's post-launch email and feels used. They mention it at the HOA. **Survival check:** Usage rule 3 (same-day logging) prevents the late entry; Usage rule 4 (launch-day sweep) guarantees the referrer personally hears "the program is live and your name carried over" on Oct 15, before any generic post-launch message can reach them; Usage rule 5 (same-day thank-you text) means the referral was acknowledged within hours, not discovered missing months later. The failure requires all three rules to fail independently. **Verdict: survivable — the rules exist precisely because this is the highest-trust moment in the campaign.**
