## HOW TO USE THIS SCRIPT

Read it until it sounds like you. Then put it down.

This script is a map, not a cage. The words below are the tested, approved language — use them until your own natural phrasing is better, and bring anything better to the daily standup so we upgrade the script for everyone.

Three rules that outrank everything on this page:

1. You are a neighbor who happens to know more about Lake Austin shorelines than anyone else on the phone that day. Not a closer. Not a telemarketer. A neighbor with useful information.
2. Real scarcity only. Numbers are published every Monday and Thursday and read at standup — never quote a slot, machine-day, crew, productive-day, or booking number out loud unless it came from that standup, and always frame it verbally as "as of [Monday/Thursday]'s count." If you don't have the current count, say "the calendar is filling" and nothing more specific.
3. Every call ends with one of three outcomes: a booked Assessment, a scheduled next touch (with a date), or a clean, gracious exit. Never a vague "I'll follow up sometime."

Timing cues are marked [0:00], [0:20] etc. Speaker notes are in (parentheses). Homeowner lines are labeled HOMEOWNER. Branch labels tell you where to jump.

The Assessment facts you will use in every scenario — learn these cold:

- $695, 100% credited toward any work the homeowner books
- On-site evaluation of bulkhead, sediment, dock structure, and drainage, with photo/video documentation
- Written prioritized scope: what must happen in this window vs. what can wait
- Rough order-of-magnitude pricing
- A reserved place in the limited production calendar
- Credit remains valid 12 months if the drawdown timing shifts
- Typical mid-size package (bulkhead + sediment) runs around $48K; full cove work can reach $95K; deposit to lock a slot is typically 15-25% of the package
- The window: October-November 2026, a 10-12 foot drop, first meaningful drawdown in nearly 10 years
- Our edge: we've committed amphibious machines for the window — most companies will be trying to rent the same machines in October; we committed in July. 3 crews planned, roughly 25 productive days planned — once machine-days are committed, we cannot add work

## SCENARIO 2 — COLD LEAD
### Skeptical, needs education. They did not ask for this call. Earn every ten seconds.

(Pre-call: confirm the address is actually Lake Austin waterfront. Know the cove name. You get one first impression — sounding like you know their shoreline is the whole game.)

### OPENING [0:00-0:20]

"Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with ATX Lakescapes — we're a shoreline construction company here in Austin. I know this is out of the blue, so I'll be quick: I'm calling Lake Austin waterfront owners about something happening this fall that directly affects your property. Can I have 90 seconds to tell you what it is? You can hang up on me if it's not relevant."

(The "you can hang up" line is deliberate. It gives them control, which is the only way a skeptic keeps listening.)

### BRANCH 2A — HOMEOWNER: "How did you get my number?"

"Straight answer: property records. Lake Austin waterfront parcels are public record, and we're contacting every shoreline owner because this affects all of you. I'm not selling anything on this call — I'm telling you about a window that's opening and closing whether either of us likes it or not. Still got 90 seconds?"

### BRANCH 2B — HOMEOWNER: "Not interested." (early, flat)

"I hear you — one sentence before I let you go, and then I'll take you off the list if you want. The lake is being lowered ten to twelve feet this fall for the first time in about ten years, and it's the only chance this decade to fix bulkheads and remove sediment affordably. If that's not relevant to you, no problem at all — want me to take you off the list?"

(If they soften: continue to EDUCATION. If they confirm the exit: thank them, log Do Not Call, move on. You protected the brand. That matters more than this lead.)

### BRANCH 2G — GATEKEEPER (house manager, assistant, or non-decision-maker answers)

"Totally understand — who handles decisions about the property itself? I'll send the one-page summary addressed to them, and what's the best way to reach them directly?

(get the name and the best channel — a name plus an email or number is a successful gatekeeper call)

Either way, I'll follow up with you Thursday so you're not chasing me down. Thanks for your help — I know this wasn't your call to take."

(Always book the Thursday follow-up with the gatekeeper too. Gatekeepers remember courtesy, and they decide whether your one-pager reaches the decision-maker's desk or the recycling bin.)

### BRANCH 2C — HOMEOWNER: "Is this about the hydrilla thing?"

"Good question — no. The City Council didn't move on the hydrilla proposal. This one's being lined up for shoreline work — which is exactly our world. For you it means the same thing either way: the water drops, the lake bed is exposed, and work that's normally impossible becomes practical."

"Here's the short version. The City and LCRA are lining up a drawdown in October and November — the water drops ten to twelve feet and stays down six to eight weeks. First meaningful one in nearly a decade.

Here's why it matters to you: after ten years of high water, most Lake Austin shorelines have issues the owner can't see — sediment built up two to four feet behind the bulkhead, tie-backs rotting, the dock structure undermined. When the water drops, all of that is exposed and — for a few weeks — fixable at a fraction of the normal cost, with equipment that can actually reach it. When the lake refills, that window closes for years.

We've locked amphibious machines for the window — most companies will be trying to rent the same machines in October; we committed in July — and we're doing Priority Assessments now. $695, fully credited toward any work you book. You get a documented, prioritized scope of your shoreline and a reserved place in a production calendar that is genuinely limited — the honest constraint is machine-days, and as of [Monday/Thursday]'s count we have [standup number] left. Once those are spoken for, we can't add work.

I'm not asking you to commit to any work. I'm asking whether you want to know what your shoreline actually looks like before the window opens."

### BRANCH 2D — HOMEOWNER: hesitant / "I need to think about it"

"That's completely reasonable — you don't know me from anyone. Here's what I'd suggest instead of a decision: let me send you the one-page summary of the Assessment and the drawdown timeline. Read it tonight, and I'll call you [day] to answer whatever it brings up. And if it's easier, drive past [nearby completed project / cove we've worked] — that's our work. No decision needed today."

(Send the one-pager within the hour. Book the callback with a specific day. Log everything.)

### CLOSE [~2:30] — always two options

"If you want to move on it, I can get you on the Assessment calendar right now — I have [day] or [day] open. Or if you'd rather start smaller, text me a few photos of your shoreline and I'll tell you straight whether a full Assessment is worth your money. Which would you rather do?"

### BRANCH 2E — HOMEOWNER: "Just send me information."

"Will do — going out within the hour. And so this doesn't just disappear into your inbox: I'll call you Thursday to see if it raised any questions. If it didn't, tell me then and I'll leave you alone. Deal?"

"Of course — and I'd never ask you to shortcut that. No ask beyond that. Would the packet first be easier, or should I just come present?"

## REBUTTAL LIBRARY — THE 10 OBJECTIONS
### Use the exact words until you own them. The psychology line is for you — never say it out loud.

---

### 1. "That's too expensive." (the work, after hearing $48K-type numbers)

"I understand — those are real numbers. Two things worth knowing. First, the same bulkhead and sediment work is typically thirty to forty percent cheaper during the drawdown, because at full water we'd be working blind, from barges, around the water instead of on the lake bed. The drawdown price is the good price. Second, you don't have to decide that number today. The Assessment is $695, credited, and it tells you exactly what's critical versus what can wait. Some clients do the critical piece this window and stage the rest. Let's find out what you're actually dealing with before either of us talks about the big number."

Psychology: reframe against the more expensive alternative, then shrink the decision back to the $695 in front of them.

---

### 2. "I need to think about it."

"Of course — it's your shoreline and your money. One thing to think with: the equipment and the calendar are being committed now, in August, not in October. 'Thinking about it' is completely fine as long as you know that the option itself has a shelf life. Here's what I'll do: I'll hold a slot for 48 hours, send you the one-pager tonight, and call you [day]. If it's a no then, it's a no — no chasing. Fair?"

Psychology: honor the autonomy fully, but attach the option's expiration to external reality, not to you.

---

### 3. "We're waiting on insurance."

"That's actually the smart order of operations — and the Assessment helps you twice. The written scope and photo documentation are exactly what an adjuster needs to see; most claims move faster with a professional scope attached than with homeowner photos. And if the claim comes through, the work is already scoped, permitted, and slotted. If it doesn't, you've lost nothing but the wait. And we structure it that way — the work gets scheduled contingent on your claim decision, and the scope we hand you is built to be adjuster-ready. Want me to note that on your file?"

Psychology: convert their delay mechanism into a reason the Assessment comes first, not last.

---

### 4. "Our HOA has to approve it."

"That comes up constantly on Lake Austin — we work with HOAs all over the lake, and here's the pattern: boards approve drawdown work faster than almost anything else, because it protects everyone's property values at once. I can put together the standard packet — scope, timeline, insurance, references from other communities — and if it helps, I'll present at a board meeting myself. The one thing I'd suggest is starting your Assessment now, because the Assessment doesn't require approval — it's just information — and it means the board decides with your scope in hand instead of a hypothetical."

Psychology: separate the decision that needs permission (the work) from the one that doesn't (the information).

---

### 5. "We're selling the property."

"Then this might matter more to you than to anyone. Agents on this lake will tell you — a documented, restored shoreline shows up in the sale price, and an undisclosed failing bulkhead kills deals in inspection. The Assessment gives you the documentation either way: a professional, photo-documented scope you can hand to your agent, either 'here's the work we completed during the drawdown' or 'here's a priced scope the buyer can act on.' Both are worth real money at the negotiating table. Doing nothing is the only option that costs you."

Psychology: reframe the shoreline from a sunk cost into a listing asset — sellers think in closing prices, not repair costs.

---

### 6. "Another company quoted me less."

"I believe it, and I'm not going to tell you their number is wrong without seeing it. Can I ask what it covers? The quotes we see that come in lower are usually working from the shoreline with conventional equipment — which in October means they can't reach the work behind the bulkhead, or they're quoting at full-water methods with a low number and change orders behind it. Our number includes the amphibious access, sediment haul-off, and the permit handling — LCRA and City of Austin — start to finish. Get our Assessment, and you'll have a written scope you can hold both quotes against. If we're genuinely higher for the same work, you'll know exactly by how much, and you can tell me to match it or walk. Fair enough?"

Psychology: never attack the competitor — arm the homeowner with the scope that exposes the comparison themselves.

---

### 7. "I'm not sure anything needs fixing."

"Honestly, you might be right — some shorelines come through fine. But here's the pattern we see: after ten years of high water, most owners genuinely don't know. The bulkhead fails from behind, the sediment builds underwater, the tie-backs rot where you can't see them. The Assessment exists precisely for people in your position: $695, fully credited, and you find out. If everything's sound, you have documentation saying so — which is worth having for insurance and resale on its own. If something's wrong, you found out in the one decade-window when fixing it is practical. Either way, you're not guessing anymore."

Psychology: sell certainty, not repair — "not knowing" is the discomfort, and the Assessment resolves it in both directions.

---

### 8. "I'll wait and see if they actually lower it — or lower it more."

"You can, and I understand the instinct. Here's the risk, stated plainly: by the time the water is visibly down, every capable crew and every amphibious machine in Texas is committed. The homeowners calling in October get told 'we can look at it next year' — and next year may be eight to ten years away. Your Assessment credit is good for twelve months, and we keep your timing flexible if the window shifts. Moving now doesn't bet on the exact dates. It just guarantees you're at the front of the line whatever the exact dates turn out to be."

Psychology: shift the perceived risk from "acting too early" to "being locked out," and remove the downside of acting early via the 12-month credit.

---

### 9. "$695 feels high for just an assessment."

"Fair — it's not a free estimate, and it's priced that way on purpose. A free estimate gets you a salesperson walking your dock for twenty minutes and a number pulled from thin air. This gets you a documented evaluation — photos, sediment depth, structural condition, drainage — a written prioritized scope, rough pricing, permit guidance, and a reserved place in a calendar that genuinely cannot take everyone. And the full $695 comes off any work you book. Most of our clients stop thinking of it as a fee and start thinking of it as a deposit that happens to come with answers. If we find nothing worth doing, it bought you certainty — and I'll tell you that to your face if it's what we find."

Psychology: reposition the fee from "charge for a sales visit" to "paid diagnosis with a credit," and signal integrity by promising honesty in the no-work outcome.

---

### 10. "I need to talk to my spouse."

"Please do — this is exactly the kind of decision that should be made together. Two ways to make it easy. One: I'll send the one-page summary plus a short video of what the Assessment actually covers, so you're not relaying a phone call from memory. Two — and most couples prefer this — we do a five-minute call, the three of us, and I answer both your questions at once. I'm free [day] evening or [day] morning. And so nobody feels rushed: I'll hold your slot until [day]. Which would you rather do?"

Psychology: treat the spouse as a missing decision-maker to include, not an objection to overcome — and never let "talk to my spouse" end without materials, a joint-call offer, and a hold date.

## HARD RULES — WHAT REPS NEVER DO

1. Never quote a slot count, machine-day count, crew count, productive-day count, or booking count that didn't come from that morning's standup — and always frame it verbally as "as of [Monday/Thursday]'s count." Fake scarcity ends this company.
2. Never promise the drawdown dates as certain. The language is "the City and LCRA are lining up," "projected," "pointing to." We sell preparation, not prophecy.
3. Never badmouth a competitor by name. Compare scopes, not companies.
4. Never quote a group discount percentage on the phone. Group terms come from Nathan, in writing.
5. Never pressure a past client past one hold + one callback. They are the referral engine for the next decade.
6. Never end a call without one of the three outcomes: booked, scheduled next touch, or gracious exit.
7. Escalate to Nathan when: a high-ticket prospect (full cove potential) wants pricing negotiation, an HOA wants group terms, or anyone mentions a structural emergency (active collapse, safety hazard). Emergencies get flagged same-day regardless of window.
8. Never claim competitor incapacity. State what we've locked, not what others can't get.
