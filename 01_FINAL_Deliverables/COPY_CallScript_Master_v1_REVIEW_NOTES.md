## LAYER 5 — DEVIL'S ADVOCATE RED TEAM REPORT

Stress test of the board-approved draft. Brutal by design.

### Five specific weaknesses

1. The script's biggest single point of failure is the standup number pipeline, not the words. Every scarcity line depends on a slot count being published accurately every morning. The day a rep quotes a stale or invented number to a homeowner who then hears a different number from a neighbor — and on Lake Austin, neighbors talk — the entire "real scarcity" brand position collapses in one cove in one afternoon. The script assumes operational discipline that doesn't exist yet (capacity dashboard is a spec, not a system).
2. Scenario 4's priority claim ("grouped properties get worked first") creates a written record of a scheduling promise Operations has never agreed to. If October sequencing favors a P1 emergency bulkhead over a grouped HOA cluster — and the Ops Playbook's P1-P4 logic says it should — the HOA will hold us to the script's language. Boards write things down.
3. Rebuttal #5 (selling the property) claims "fifty thousand dollars or more" of sale-price impact. That figure traces to the cheat sheet's internal assertion, not to any documented comp or appraisal. A homeowner's agent will challenge it, and the rep has no citation. It's the one number in the script most likely to be repeated back to us as a quote.
4. The cold scenario has no branch for gatekeeper encounters (house manager, assistant, spouse who is not the decision-maker and says so). Cold lists of $2M+ properties will hit gatekeepers at meaningful rates; reps will improvise, and improvised gatekeeper handling is where compliance and tone failures happen.
5. The 48-hour hold mechanic (Scenarios 1, 2; Rebuttals 2, 10) has no defined inventory rule. If three reps each hold slots for 48 hours during the August 8 surge, the published "slots remaining" number becomes fiction — holds inflate apparent scarcity while conversions lag, and the capacity dashboard oversells. Holds need a cap and an expiry sweep.

### Three concrete improvements (applied to FINAL)

1. Added Placeholder 1 + Hard Rule 1 making standup the sole source of scarcity numbers, plus the "if you don't have the number, say 'the calendar is filling' and nothing more specific" fallback line — a scripted behavior for the exact failure mode in weakness #1. (Also recommend — outside this document — that Operations cap active 48-hour holds per rep and auto-expire them at the daily standup; flagged to Sales Engineer for Batch 3.5 dashboard spec.)
2. Scenario 4 scheduling-priority language now conditioned on Operations confirmation (Placeholder 7), and the group-priority sentence reframed as "easier to schedule, which means priority as the calendar fills" rather than a hard promise. The resale claim in Rebuttal #5 was reframed around documentation value at this stage, but the unsourced fifty-thousand-dollar figure remained in the text — the external Devil's Advocate round (below) flagged it and the figure has now been removed entirely.
3. Gatekeeper handling for cold calls was addressed only as a pre-call note (confirm the decision-maker's name before dialing) plus an August 4 training item — a full scripted gatekeeper branch was NOT included in the script body at this stage, and this improvement note incorrectly implied otherwise. The external Devil's Advocate round (below) caught the discrepancy against weakness #4; it is now fixed for real as BRANCH 2G in the FINAL file.

### One worst-case scenario

August 8 launch week. A rep, under quota pressure, tells a cold prospect "we're the only company with amphibious equipment" AND quotes a specific slots-remaining number from memory. The prospect's neighbor is a past client who was told a different number the same week — and who also knows a competing contractor now claiming they have amphibious access too. The two compare notes at a dock party. The thread lands on the neighborhood Facebook group and Nextdoor: "ATX Lakescapes is running a pressure script with fake scarcity." In a market of a few hundred high-value waterfront households, one credible public screenshot of contradictory numbers costs more trust than the entire warm list can rebuild in one season — and it lands the same week local media (KVUE/KXAN pitches) is being courted. This is why Hard Rules 1-2 exist and why certification before August 4 is non-negotiable. The script cannot prevent a rep from lying; only the morning-number discipline, the hold-expiry sweep, and Nathan auditing call recordings in week one can.

Red team sign-off: concerns documented, improvements applied where the script itself can carry them, operational dependencies flagged to Batch 3 (funnel/CRM/dashboard) and Phase 1 ownership.
