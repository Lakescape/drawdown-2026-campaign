## 1. WHAT CHANGED FROM THE SOURCE DRAFT (Sales Copy Suite §5)

The source Section 5 was a starting point — a greeting plus five answers, several of which broke campaign rules. Material changes:

1. **Greeting handled — initially by omission, corrected in the external round.** The source opener asserted "The Lake Austin drawdown is projected for October–November 2026" as a flat statement with an unhedged date; the first FINAL dropped the greeting entirely rather than fixing it. (The original wording of this note — "Fixed the unhedged greeting" — was inaccurate: the greeting was absent, not fixed. Corrected per external red-team fix F13.) A hedged two-sentence Greeting section is now part of the FINAL, and FAQ 3 leads with "Nothing is official until it's official — the City is exploring a drawdown," consistent with the email suite's Global Rule 2 and the call script's Hard Rule 2.
2. **Retired the source's pricing points ($48K / $95K+) in favor of hedged ranges ($15–25K / $35–55K / $65–95K).** The source stated "$48K" and "$95K+" as near-fixed numbers in a channel where a chatter screenshot is forever. Ranges are what the Master Plan's offer stack supports (bulkhead ~$35K, sediment ~$18K, dock ~$12K as components; $48K typical mid-size; $95K full-cove anchor), and ranges survive being quoted back at us. "Depends on what we find" is attached every time.
3. **Deleted "essentially free."** The source said the Assessment is "essentially free if you move forward." A $695 fee is not free, and a $5M homeowner hears that phrase as a sales trick. Now: "the $695 is fully credited, so if you move forward, the Assessment costs you nothing" — the call script's framing, kept honest.
4. **Added the two missing FAQs.** The source had no HOA answer and no booking answer. FAQ 6 mirrors call script Scenario 4 (board packet, presentation offer, shared mobilization, $695-per-property credit unchanged, no quoted discount percentage). FAQ 7 offers three entry paths — book in chat, call/text Nathan's line, or the photo-assessment path — matching the call script's two-option close philosophy.
5. **Added "What if the drawdown is delayed?" kill-switch alignment.** Source's version ("apply it to pre-drawdown work or hold it for the next window") invented a "pre-drawdown work" application that no other approved document promises. Now: 12-month credit validity + calendar place carries over — exactly what the call script (Rebuttal 8) and email kill-switch promise, nothing more.
6. **Enforced the format spec.** Every response is two sentences of answer plus one booking question. The source ran 3–4 sentences and ended several answers with no question at all.
7. **Added a Usage Block and placeholder table** — response-time SLA, escalation triggers (same as call script Hard Rule 7), scarcity verification, CRM logging, after-hours capture gap flagged.

## 4. DEVIL'S ADVOCATE RED TEAM — LAYER 5

### Five specific weaknesses (found and dispositioned)

1. **FAQ 2's ranges can still be quoted back as a floor.** A chatter hears "$15–25K" and anchors at $15K for their bulkhead; the real scope comes in at $40K and now we're "more expensive than you said in chat." → *Mitigation:* the answer opens with "it depends on what we find" and closes on the Assessment as the source of "your real number" — the ranges are explicitly framed as not-their-number. Reps are trained (Usage rule 2) not to elaborate tiers in chat beyond what's written.
2. **FAQ 3's "we're preparing as if it's coming" could be read as inside knowledge.** If the drawdown is cancelled, a screenshot of us "preparing as if" plus selling Assessments could be spun as selling against a window we knew might not open. → *Mitigation:* the same sentence contains the hedge ("Nothing is official until it's official"), and FAQ 5 makes the carry-over explicit — the two answers together are the defense: we disclosed uncertainty and removed the financial downside of acting early.
3. **FAQ 6's board-packet offer creates a document trail before group terms exist.** Nathan has not yet set group discount terms in writing (call script placeholder 6). A board member who says "yes, send the packet" forces a deadline on an unowned asset. → *Mitigation:* Global Rule 6 bars quoting percentages; the placeholder table assigns group terms to Nathan in writing, same rule as call script Hard Rule 4. Reps offer the call (FAQ 6's question), not a date for the packet.
4. **The 5-minute SLA is easy to promise and hard to keep.** Chat lives on the website 24/7; the sales team works 9–6 weekdays. A chat answered in 90 minutes under a 5-minute internal standard trains the homeowner that we overpromise — the exact opposite of the brand. → *Mitigation:* Usage rule 1 scopes the SLA to staffed hours; the after-hours capture gap is flagged to the orchestrator rather than papered over.
5. **FAQ 7's photo path can leak into free consulting.** "Send a few photos and I'll tell you honestly whether it's worth $695" invites owners to extract a free mini-assessment and vanish. → *Mitigation:* this is deliberate and inherited from the approved call script close — the photo path converts skeptics at higher rates than it leaks, because the honest verdict ("yes, you need this") is our best sales asset. Reps answer the photo with a verdict plus a booking question, never a scope.

### Three concrete improvements (applied)

1. **Added Global Rule 1's human-only mandate and the no-visible-placeholder rule** — the source draft had brackets in a channel where an auto-responder might serve them raw.
2. **Added the escalation triggers to the Usage Block (rule 4)** — identical to call script Hard Rule 7, so a chatter mentioning an active collapse gets the same-day flag regardless of channel. Safety consistency across channels was a gap in the source.
3. **Added Usage rule 6 (three-outcome logging)** — chat previously had no conversion discipline; now every thread ends booked, scheduled, or graciously closed, same as a call, and feeds the same nurture/suppression infrastructure.

### One worst-case scenario

**A chatter is a journalist or a competitor's principal, screenshotted FAQ 2 and FAQ 3 run in a neighborhood Facebook group or a local news story after the drawdown is cancelled: "Lakescapes quoted $95K jobs against a drawdown that never happened."** *Contained by:* (a) FAQ 2's ranges are component-grounded (Master Plan offer stack) and framed as dependent on findings — the screenshot shows hedging, not a quote; (b) FAQ 3's first sentence is the disclosure — "Nothing is official until it's official" is on the record in our own words; (c) FAQ 5's carry-over means no homeowner lost money, so there is no victim for the story; (d) no invented scarcity number exists anywhere in the file to be debunked. The campaign's defense, again, is that the hedged-language rule was followed before it was needed.
