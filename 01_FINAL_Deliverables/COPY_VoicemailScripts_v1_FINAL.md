## HOW TO USE THESE SCRIPTS (READ BEFORE THE FIRST CALL BLOCK)

A voicemail is not a shrunken sales call. It is a 15–20 second knock on the door that makes the text and the email land. Each script below does one job: earn a callback or a YES reply. Nothing else.

**Voice — whose name is on the message.** Voicemails are left by reps as themselves. If Nathan wants his own voice on a VM, he records it personally — never scripted for reps. Reps dial 40–50 numbers a day; Nathan does not. A past client who knows Nathan's voice catching a rep impersonating him on a forwardable recording is a brand-destroying moment — and on Lake Austin, neighbors talk. Written channels (email, SMS) go out under Nathan's name because Nathan writes words; voice belongs to whoever is speaking.

Five rules that outrank every word on this page:

1. **Hedged drawdown language only.** The City is "lining up" / "exploring" the drawdown; the window is "projected." Never say the drawdown "is scheduled" or state unhedged dates on a recording that can be saved and forwarded. We sell preparation, not prophecy.
2. **Real scarcity only.** Quote a slot or calendar number only from the current standup count, always framed as "as of [Monday/Thursday]'s count." If you don't have the current count, say "the calendar is filling" and nothing more. Never improvise a number on someone's voicemail — it lives forever.
3. **Three voicemails maximum per contact per cycle, never back-to-back.** Between VM1 and VM2, and between VM2 and VM3, there must be at least one live-attempt call where you do NOT leave a message (or the scheduled SMS touch). Three messages then silence. A fourth voicemail is harassment wearing a name tag.
4. **Weekday windows.** Calls (and therefore voicemails) happen weekdays 9:00 AM–6:00 PM CT only; Monday calls start at 10:00 AM. No weekend voicemails. Ever.
5. **Live answer beats the script.** If they pick up, you are in the Master Call Script, not this file. Never say "I was just going to leave you a voicemail."

**Delivery:** Stand up. Smile — it is audible. Speak at roughly 140 words per minute, slower than feels natural. Say the phone number like you're reading it to someone holding a pen. Record yourself once before your first call block and time it: if you run past 20 seconds, cut words — do not speed up.

**Live-dialed calls only.** These scripts are for voicemail reached on a live-dialed call. No ringless voicemail drops without Nathan's written sign-off and legal review — TCPA exposure on a $5M homeowner is the single biggest legal risk in this campaign.

## VM1 — FIRST TOUCH (PAST CLIENT)

**When:** First unanswered call to a past client, the day after the A1 email + SMS touch. They know us. Sound like it.

**Word count: 50 | Read time: ~19–20 seconds**

> "Hey [First], it's [Rep] from Nathan's team at ATX Lakescapes. The City's lining up the first real Lake Austin drawdown in about a decade — October into November — and past clients get first shot at the assessment calendar. It's $695, fully credited to any work. I just texted you. [Number]. Talk soon."

**Delivery notes:**
- You are [Rep], not Nathan. Past clients know Nathan's voice — say your own name like it belongs to you, because on this lake it will.
- "I just texted you" must be literally true — verify the SMS A1 sent and delivered before the call block. If it didn't, swap the line to: "I'll text you the details right after this."
- Say the number once, slowly. Past clients already have the text with your number; the voicemail just points at it.

## VM1 — FIRST TOUCH (COLD LEAD VARIANT)

**When:** First unanswered call to a cold lead, the day after the B1 email. They did not ask to hear from you. Identity first, no pitch.

**Word count: 48 | Read time: ~19–20 seconds**

> "Hi [First], this is [Rep] from Nathan Menkin's crew at ATX Lakescapes. The City and LCRA are exploring a drawdown this fall, first in about a decade. I emailed you a short note about it yesterday — happy to send the one-page summary if it's relevant. [Number]. That's [Number]."

**Delivery notes:**
- The number is said twice. Voicemail transcription mangles numbers, and a cold contact has no text from you to fall back on.
- "I emailed you a short note about it yesterday" refers to the B1 email itself — true by cadence, because B1 goes out the day before this call block. The one-page summary is offered, not claimed as sent: B1 only offers it on reply, so never say you already sent it. If their B1 bounced, swap the clause to: "I'd like to send you a short note and the one-page summary."
- "Happy to send the one-page summary if it's relevant" does the same job as "no pitch on a voicemail" — it gives a skeptic control, which is the only way a skeptic calls back.

## VM2 — FOLLOW-UP (BOTH LISTS)

**When:** Second unanswered call, after at least one no-message live attempt since VM1.

**Word count: 47 | Read time: ~19–20 seconds**

> "Hey [First], [Rep] again — Nathan's team at ATX Lakescapes. Quick update: our amphibious machines are committed for the window, and as of [Monday/Thursday]'s count the calendar is filling toward a waitlist. Want to know what your shoreline looks like before the water drops? Now's the easy time. [Number]."

**Delivery notes:**
- If the standup published a slots-remaining number and you have it in front of you, you may say: "...and as of [Monday/Thursday]'s count we have [standup number] assessment slots left before the waitlist." If you do not have the current count, the version above is the whole claim. Do not split the difference.
- "Committed for the window" is the truth — we committed in July. Never say the machines are "being locked down"; that's stale and sounds in-progress.

---

## VM3 — FINAL (BOTH LISTS)

**When:** Last call of the cycle. **Monday–Thursday only**, and [Friday, Date] must be the real, standup-confirmed waitlist cutoff for that list. Never leave VM3 on a Friday — "after Friday" a week out kills the message; leaving it Mon–Thu keeps it true and near.

**Word count: 40 | Read time: ~15–16 seconds**

> "[First], [Rep] at ATX Lakescapes — this is my last call about the drawdown. New assessments move to the waitlist after [Friday, Date]. If you want in: [Number]. If not, no hard feelings — I hope the fall treats your shoreline well."

**Delivery notes:**
- This is a clean, dignified exit, not a threat. The tone is the same as B4 in the email suite: in or out, both are fine.
- After VM3: silence. The contact moves to the nurture track. No fourth message, no "just checking once more."

## KILL-SWITCH VOICEMAIL (DRAWDOWN DELAY / CANCELLATION)

If the City/LCRA shifts the window — a low-probability, critical-impact risk — stop the cadence immediately. On the next scheduled touch, leave this instead of any scripted VM:

**Word count: 49 | Read time: ~19–20 seconds**

> "Hey [First], [Rep] at ATX Lakescapes, from Nathan's team — quick update on the drawdown: the City has shifted the timing. Nothing is lost on your end — your assessment and credit carry straight over to the new window. We'll reach out the moment new dates are lined up. Questions: [Number]."

Then rebuild every cadence around the new window dates, consistent with the SMS and email kill-switch pivots.

## WHEN THEY CALL BACK

A returned call is the whole point — do not fumble it.

1. Open with: "Hey [First], thanks for calling back." Then pick up the Master Call Script at VALUE + SCARCITY for their scenario (past client / cold / referral).
2. Reference the channel they know: "You got my text/email — the short version is…" Never make them re-explain why they called.
3. If the callback comes to the main line instead of the rep's cell, the rep who left the VM gets the handoff with the contact's card already open — the homeowner heard THAT rep's voice, and continuity is the trust.
4. Log the callback against the voicemail in the CRM — VM callback rate is a measured KPI (working target 15%+, uncalibrated — to be set by Nathan after week 1 data), and we cannot improve what we don't count.

## USAGE BLOCK (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

1. **Cadence pairing.** VM1 lands the day AFTER the email/SMS first touch, never before. A voicemail referencing a text that hasn't arrived reads as a robocall and burns the number's reputation.
2. **Verify the pair before the call block.** Confirm SMS delivered (past clients) or email delivered (cold) for every name on the day's call sheet. Undelivered pair → use the swap lines in the delivery notes.
3. **Count discipline.** The [standup number] swap-in for VM2 comes only from the current Monday/Thursday published count, read at standup, quoted as "as of [day]'s count." Reps never reuse a stale count on a recording.
4. **VM3 truth gate.** [Friday, Date] is the standup-confirmed cutoff for that specific list (past-client and cold cutoffs can differ — see the email suite's waitlist-integrity note). Sales lead confirms both cutoffs at standup; an unconfirmed date means VM3 doesn't get left.
5. **Suppression is shared.** A STOP, NO, or "take me off your list" on any channel — text, email, or a live call — ends all voicemails for that contact immediately and permanently.
6. **Recording awareness.** Every voicemail is a forwardable artifact. If a line would embarrass us played back at an HOA meeting, it doesn't get left. (Every line above is written to survive that test.)
7. **No ringless drops.** Live-dialed calls only, absent Nathan's written sign-off and legal review.
8. **Reps voice their own name.** No rep ever says "it's Nathan" or "Nathan's team" without "from" in front of it. Nathan's own voicemails, if any, are recorded by Nathan personally.
