## HOW TO USE THIS TEMPLATE (READ BEFORE ANY PROPOSAL GOES OUT)

1. **One client, one proposal, built from their actual report.** Every [Finding] below comes from that client's Priority Assessment report — if the report isn't in front of you, the proposal doesn't get built. A generic finding on a $5M property reads as mail-merge and kills the neighbor illusion (same data discipline as Email Sequence C).
2. **Hedged drawdown language only.** The City is "exploring" the drawdown; the window is "projected" / "being lined up" / "pointing to October–November." A proposal is a signature document — the hedging matters more here than anywhere else. We sell preparation, not prophecy. Never state the drawdown as certain, and never state unhedged dates.
3. **Real scarcity only.** The approved structural numbers are: **2 amphibious machines locked, ~25 working days in-window, committed in July.** Live counts appear only as [X] placeholders filled from the most recent Mon/Thu published count and framed as "as of [day]'s count." Never improvise a number on a document the client will keep.
4. **The $695 credit is visible on every option.** It appears as a line-item deduction on all three tiers — never buried, never mentioned only once. The client should be able to find their credit in under five seconds from any page.
5. **The client is in control.** No countdown language, no "act now," no manufactured pressure. The honest mechanics — the calendar hold, the deposit deadline — are stated as facts with real dates, not as sales devices. If a sentence would sound like pressure at a BBQ, cut it.
6. **Reps sign as themselves.** The representative signature line carries the rep's own name and title. Nathan's name is never scripted into anyone else's signature.
7. **Ranges are guidance, prices are theirs.** The $15–25K / $35–55K / $65–95K ranges below are rep-facing orientation for tier mapping. The client's proposal carries their actual scoped price as $[amount] — never send a proposal quoting only a range as the price.
8. **No unfilled placeholders except deliberate ones.** [X] standup counts are filled at send time. Everything else is filled at build time. A visible bracket in a signed-path document is worse than a slow follow-up.

## PAGE 4 — HOW TO CHOOSE

There's no wrong option here — only the one that fits your property and your plans. How we'd think about it if it were our shoreline:

**Choose STABILIZE if** the report shows a critical structural issue and you want it handled correctly without committing to the full scope this window. The rest of the findings stay documented and priced; your credit and this scope carry forward 12 months, so staging costs you nothing but time.

**Choose RESTORE if** you want the shoreline genuinely fixed while the water is down — bulkhead, sediment, drainage — and the property is staying in the family. Most Lake Austin owners land here, because the middle scope is where the drawdown does the most work per dollar: at full water, the same job means working blind, from barges, around the water instead of on the lake bed.

**Choose TRANSFORM if** the cove itself is the asset — you're holding long-term, or heading toward a sale in the next few years and want the shoreline to show like the rest of the property. A documented, restored shoreline shows up in sale price; an undisclosed failing bulkhead shows up in inspection.

**Not ready to choose any of them?** That's a legitimate outcome too. Your assessment report and $695 credit are good for 12 months, and if you tell us it's a pass, we'll close the file gracefully and check in after the window. The only option we'd argue against is deciding nothing by default — the calendar is the one thing that doesn't wait, and the constraint is real: we have two amphibious machines committed for the window and roughly 25 working days in it. As of [day]'s count, [X] machine-days remain uncommitted, and your preliminary slot holds through [Calendar-Hold Date].

**Want to talk it through first?** Call or text [Rep Name] directly at [Rep direct line] — thirty minutes, your report in hand, no charge and no pressure. If a spouse, partner, or property manager should be part of that conversation, we'll set up a time that works for everyone.
