## DEVIL'S ADVOCATE — RED TEAM

### Five specific weaknesses

1. **DA-1 — The calendar-hold date can still read as manufactured pressure** on a $5M homeowner if the rep picks a short or arbitrary date. Mitigation: usage rule and placeholder table require the hold date to be the real slot-release date from the sales lead/CRM, consistent with what C2/C3 already told the client. A date the client has seen twice before is information; a date that appears for the first time in a contract is pressure.
2. **DA-2 — Tier range labels risk anchoring.** A client whose genuine scope is $18K may feel steered toward Option 2 because the middle tier is framed as "where most owners land." Mitigation: the rep-side note ("reshape the tier to fit the shoreline — never stretch a shoreline to fit a tier") and the exec-summary recommendation sentence, which is required to be honest even when the honest answer is Option 1. Sales Engineer held at ACCEPTABLE on this point; residual risk accepted with the honesty requirement as the control.
3. **DA-3 — Warranty terms (1-year / 2-year) were inherited from the source draft and Master Plan value stack but never approved in writing by Nathan.** A proposal is a promise; unapproved warranty terms in a signed document are a real liability. Mitigation: bracketed as [1 year / 2 years] with an explicit "confirm with Nathan before first send" rule and placeholder-table entry. Blocking before first live send, not before FINAL status.
4. **DA-4 — "Refund, roll, or credit" is contract-adjacent language.** It's the approved campaign language and must stay verbatim, but this is the first deliverable where it appears in a document the client signs. The mechanics of "roll" (to what schedule, at what priority) are undefined anywhere in the campaign docs. Mitigation: flagged to Nathan/legal for a read of the Terms page before first signature; "roll" mechanics definition flagged to Operations. Not a copy change — the language is approved — a process gap.
5. **DA-5 — Option 3's "property-value impact documentation" is an unowned asset.** Same class of gap as the one-page summary flagged in the Call Script and Live Chat FAQ placeholder lists. If Option 3 sends before that asset exists, we promise a deliverable we can't produce. Mitigation: placeholder-table entry with flag-to-orchestrator instruction.

### Three concrete improvements (all applied to FINAL)

1. **Credit as line item on every option** — the –$695 appears in the exec-summary table, all three pricing blocks, and the acceptance page checkboxes (from Round 2 fix; confirmed as the single highest-leverage improvement in the document).
2. **"How to Choose" page with an exit ramp** — the explicit "not ready to choose any of them" path, which paradoxically strengthens the close: the only option we argue against is "deciding nothing by default," and we attach that argument to the real machine-day arithmetic, not to pressure.
3. **"What happens after you sign" timeline** — six numbered steps from deposit confirmation to as-built record, converting the signature from a leap of faith into a known process (directly answers the brief requirement and mirrors the "you never chase the crew" value-stack promise).

### Worst-case scenario

**The drawdown is cancelled after deposits are signed.** Dozens of signed proposals carrying "refund, roll, or credit" activate simultaneously. The template's exposure: "roll" is undefined (DA-4), and every proposal also carries a confirmed-in-writing mobilization expectation the ops team must now unwind client by client. The protections that survive the worst case: the window-shift terms are verbatim-approved and visible at signature (no client can claim they weren't told); the 12-month credit has a defined life; and nothing in the document promised the window as certain — the hedged language that felt conservative in July is what keeps a cancelled drawdown from becoming a refund-and-reputation crisis. Residual action: Nathan/legal read of Terms (DA-4) and a defined "roll" mechanic are the two items that must close before the first signature, not after.
