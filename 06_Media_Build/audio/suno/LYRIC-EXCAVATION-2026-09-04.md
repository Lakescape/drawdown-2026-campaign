## Cold read — all four songs, what they point at

| Song | Points attention at | |
|---|---|---|
| A The Window | the event — announcement, window, deadline | explains |
| B Work the Bed | the crew's rhythm — iron, trailer, long day | performs |
| C Home Water | the lake as inheritance | reveres |
| D Dry Lake Line (Suno) | the bed's physical texture — glass, wire, clay | observes |

**One consequential absence, shared by all four: the owner is not in the song.**

Every line is the lake or the crew. The homeowner — the person who will actually
pay for this — appears once, obliquely, in A's *"whatever's under your dock
tonight / has been down there since you bought her"*, and never returns. Three of
four songs are a crew singing about itself. Suno's is better written and has the
same hole.

That single line is also the best line in any of the four, which is the tell.

## The connection worth testing

Researched the ordinary framing first: this reads as a *construction* story —
window, machines, schedule. That framing is what produced all four songs.

**The non-obvious neighbor: it is a disclosure story.** Ten years of water hid
something a person owns. The drawdown is not a construction opportunity, it is a
**reveal** — structurally closer to an inspection, an X-ray, or clearing out a
house you inherited than to a build.

- **Shared mechanism:** a thing you own, continuously, that you have never seen —
  and a bounded interval in which seeing it is possible at all.
- **What it changes:** the song stops being about our capability and becomes about
  the listener's property. "We work the bed" is a vendor's sentence. "You have
  never seen the bottom of what you own" is the customer's.
- **Where it fails:** an inspection frame can tip into fear-selling, which the
  campaign's own red-team notes were written to prevent. The drawdown is real and
  announced; the *dread* is not something to manufacture.
- **Smallest test:** one verse and one chorus rewritten in second person, against
  the existing B arrangement. One variable, one comparison.

## The experiment — B, second person

Same material, same meter, same arrangement. Only the point of view moves.

```
[Verse 1]
Ten years you have owned it
And ten years you have not looked
There is a floor beneath your boat lift
You have never put a foot on

[Chorus]
Now the bed comes up
Scrape it, haul it, cover it
Now the bed comes up
And then the water does
```

Tradeoff, stated plainly: this is colder than B. It loses the crew camaraderie
that makes B pleasant to listen to, and it asks something of the listener instead
of entertaining them. It is more likely to convert and less likely to be enjoyed.
That is a real choice, not a strict improvement.
