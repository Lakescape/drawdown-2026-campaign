## Three things this corrects

1. **The runbook's capture window is wrong.** `EARTH_PRO_RUNBOOK.md` says the
   lake "sat at its fully lowered floor roughly the last two weeks of that
   window — target Feb 1–13, 2017." In fact the floor was hit **Jan 13**, and by
   **Feb 13 the lake was back to 491.14 — essentially full.** Only Feb 1–6 was
   still at floor. Chasing February imagery was chasing a refilled lake.
2. **It didn't matter anyway** — Google flew Lake Austin exactly once during the
   drawdown, on 1/8/2017. Verified across five placemarks spanning both banks
   and out to Steiner Ranch. That single pass is the entire aerial record.
3. **The aerial is mid-drawdown, not bottom.** On 1/8 the lake was 5.95 ft down
   of an eventual 10.83 ft — **55% of the way**. It fell another 4.88 ft after
   the photo was taken. That is why the canal grid still reads water-filled.

## Two things this corroborates — use these

The one-pager's projections are not guesses. The last actual event backs them:

- **"projected ten-to-twelve-foot drop"** → 2017 actual was **10.83 ft**.
- **"roughly six to eight weeks"** → 2017 ran Jan 2 → Feb 20, about **7 weeks**.

## The caption this unlocks

> **January 8, 2017.** The lake was 5.9 feet down.
> It fell another 4.9 feet after this photo was taken.

Honest, specific, and it makes the viewer extrapolate past the frame instead of
doubting it — which is stronger than the dramatic image we cannot obtain.
