## Specs (set once, don't change between shots)

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | **Vertical 9:16** | These are reels. Horizontal footage cannot be recovered to vertical without destroying framing. |
| Resolution / fps | 4K 30fps if available, else 1080p 30 | Headroom for reframing + the 4K/HDR-off note below |
| HDR | **OFF** | iPhone HDR/Dolby Vision fights the grade and looks wrong on most social players |
| Lock | **AE/AF lock** before rolling (tap-and-hold) | Prevents mid-shot exposure hunting, the #1 killer of usable phone b-roll |
| Length per take | **12–15 seconds minimum** | Scripts need 2–3s; the extra gives edit handles |
| Takes per setup | **2** (one static, one with the move) | Cheap insurance, no second trip |
| Audio | Roll it, don't rely on it | Real machine sound is gold; wind noise is not recoverable |

**Do not:** zoom while rolling · shoot into direct sun · add any phone "cinematic mode" or filter · film people's faces without asking.

## SCRIPT 2 — "Meet the Machine" (3 shots)

### 2A — tracks leave water, bite into mud *(the hook — most important shot of the set)*
- **Frame:** low and close on the undercarriage, camera near ground level, pontoon floats and flotation tracks filling most of frame. Water in the lower third at the start.
- **Action:** machine moves from shallow water up onto exposed mud in **one continuous motion**. Do not cut mid-move.
- **Move:** static. Let the machine cross the frame.
- **Must be visible:** the pontoons, and the fact that it came *out of the water*. That is the entire point of the shot.
- **Overlay it feeds:** "It floats. It crawls."

### 2B — track macro
- **Frame:** extreme close-up, one flotation track plate, cleats visible, mud squeezing between them.
- **Action:** slow track rotation. Machine can be stationary and just tracking in place.
- **Move:** static, tripod or braced.
- **Watch for:** keep real colour — silver steel, olive-brown mud, yellow paint at frame edge. Do not shoot this one in flat shade or it goes grey.

### 2C — wide, machine + dock + Hill Country
- **Frame:** wide. Machine working the shoreline, **a dock clearly in frame**, limestone bank and cedar/live-oak hills behind.
- **Action:** machine working — any real task, doesn't need to be dramatic.
- **Move:** slow push-in (walk forward smoothly) OR static. Both takes.
- **Must be visible:** low waterline — the pale exposed band between water and vegetation. That band *is* the drawdown story.
- **Overlay it feeds:** "We locked two of them. In July."

## SCRIPT 5 — "Two Machines" (2 shots)

### 5A — both machines together *(the proof shot)*
- **Frame:** both amphibious units in one frame, side by side, staged and clean, booms down.
- **Move:** slow lateral drift left→right that *reveals* the second machine. Static take as backup.
- **Must be visible:** unmistakably **two** machines, both clearly amphibious. This shot is the literal evidence for "two machines locked."
- **Overlay it feeds:** "Two machines." / "~25 working days."

### 5B — staged readiness, no machine
- **Frame:** quiet wide of the prepared shoreline — matting, rope, staged gear, **no machine in frame**.
- **Move:** locked off, near-still.
- **Feel:** calm, waiting. This is the breath before the work.

## Bonus if it's already there (30 seconds, no setup)

### 6A — waterline stain on rock/bulkhead
The single most useful drawdown image you can own: the horizontal stain band showing where water used to sit. Static, close, no machine. Feeds Scripts 1/4/6 later.
